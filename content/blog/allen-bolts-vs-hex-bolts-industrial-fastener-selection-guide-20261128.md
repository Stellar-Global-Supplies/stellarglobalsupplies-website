# Allen Bolts vs Hex Bolts: How to Choose for Industrial Assembly

Allen bolts and hex bolts are both common threaded fasteners, but their head and drive designs affect tool access, clearance and assembly. Allen bolts use an internal hex socket operated with an Allen key or hex bit. Hex bolts have an external hexagonal head operated with a spanner or socket.

For industrial buyers, the right choice depends on the drawing, available tool access, joint design, required standard, size, grade, material and finish—not only the bolt name.

## What are Allen bolts?

Allen bolts, commonly called socket head cap screws, have a cylindrical head with a hexagonal recess. The internal drive allows the fastener to be turned with a hex key or socket bit.

The Stellar Global Supplies catalogue lists Allen bolts to ISO 4762 / DIN 912, with sizes from M3 to M48 and listed grades/materials including 8.8, 12.9, SS 304 and SS 316. Confirm the exact size, grade, material and finish required for your drawing before ordering.

Common selection considerations include:

- Internal-hex tool access and the clearance needed above the head.
- Whether the cylindrical head fits the surrounding component geometry.
- Required screw length, thread engagement and mating thread.
- The specified standard, grade, material and coating.

## What are hex bolts?

Hex bolts have an external six-sided head. They are typically tightened with a spanner or socket and are used in a wide range of structural, machinery and general engineering connections.

The catalogue lists hex bolts to IS 1364 and DIN 931 / DIN 933, sizes M6 to M100, and grades/materials including 4.6, 8.8, 10.9, SS 304 and SS 316. Listed thread options include fully threaded and part-threaded, with finishes including BZP, HDG and self-colour. Confirm the required option and availability for the order.

Common selection considerations include:

- Clearance around the external hex head for the tool.
- Whether full thread or partial thread is specified.
- Grip length, thread engagement and mating components.
- Required standard, grade, material, coating and corrosion environment.

## Allen bolts vs hex bolts: practical differences

- **Drive access:** Allen bolts need access for an internal hex key or bit. Hex bolts need clearance around the outside of the head for a spanner or socket.
- **Head geometry:** Socket-head screws have a cylindrical head; hex bolts have an external hex head. Check the drawing and surrounding component clearances.
- **Assembly tools:** Choose the drive that fits the tools and access available during installation and maintenance.
- **Specifications:** Do not assume two bolts are interchangeable because the thread diameter is the same. Standard, head dimensions, length, thread, grade and finish may differ.
- **Joint design:** Use the fastener type specified by the drawing or engineering requirement. Check the complete joint, including mating thread, washers, nuts, loading and tightening method.

## When should buyers consider Allen bolts?

Allen bolts may be a suitable option when the assembly specifies a socket-head fastener, when a cylindrical head fits the design, or when the installation tool can reach the internal socket.

The catalogue lists applications for Allen bolts including CNC machine assembly, hydraulic manifolds, automotive components, robotics and precision instruments. These are application examples, not a blanket suitability guarantee; confirm the design, load, material and grade requirements.

## When should buyers consider hex bolts?

Hex bolts may be appropriate when the design calls for an external hex head and the installation area provides room for a spanner or socket. The catalogue lists applications including structural steel joints, flange connections, heavy machinery, bridge fabrication and general engineering.

The correct grade, thread, length, finish and joint requirements must be confirmed for the particular application.

## Fastener selection checklist for an RFQ

Prepare the following information before requesting a quotation:

- Fastener type and head/drive: socket head (Allen) or external hex.
- Standard or drawing reference, such as ISO 4762 / DIN 912 or IS 1364 / DIN 931 / DIN 933.
- Diameter, thread pitch and length.
- Full-thread or part-thread requirement, where applicable.
- Grade and material, including stainless grade if specified.
- Finish or coating requirement and operating environment.
- Quantity, application and delivery location.
- Required documents, inspection or acceptance criteria.
- Any mating nut, washer, tool-access or installation constraints.

A complete specification helps avoid quoting a fastener that has the correct diameter but the wrong head, thread, grade or finish.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Allen bolts and hex bolts the same?

They are both threaded fasteners, but their head and drive designs differ. Allen bolts use an internal hex socket; hex bolts use an external hex head. The specified fastener should match the drawing and assembly requirements.

### What standard is used for Allen bolts?

The catalogue lists ISO 4762 / DIN 912 socket head cap screws. Confirm the exact standard, dimensions, grade and material on the requirement.

### What standards are listed for hex bolts?

The catalogue lists IS 1364 and DIN 931 / DIN 933. Confirm the exact standard and thread configuration needed.

### Can an Allen bolt replace a hex bolt?

Do not substitute based only on nominal diameter or thread. Head dimensions, drive, length, grade, material, joint design and tool access may differ. Obtain engineering approval if the drawing specifies a particular fastener.

### What details should I send for pricing?

Send the standard or drawing, diameter, thread, length, grade/material, finish, quantity, application and delivery location. If a drawing is available, include it with the enquiry so the specification can be checked.
