# MS vs Stainless Steel Sheets: How to Choose the Right Material

Choosing between mild steel (MS) sheet and stainless steel (SS) sheet is an important procurement decision. The right material depends on where the sheet will be used, how it will be fabricated, the surface finish required, exposure to moisture or chemicals, and the total cost over its service life.

For fabrication shops, OEMs, contractors and procurement teams, selecting material based only on the initial price can lead to avoidable coating, maintenance or replacement costs. This guide outlines practical differences to help you shortlist a material before requesting a quotation.

## MS sheet vs SS sheet: quick comparison

Instead of a table, compare the key factors below:

- **Typical purchase consideration:** MS is often chosen when initial material cost and fabrication requirements are key. SS is often considered when corrosion resistance, hygiene, appearance or finish matter.
- **Corrosion behaviour:** MS can rust when exposed to moisture and oxygen; protective coating or maintenance may be needed. SS generally has better corrosion resistance due to its chromium-rich passive surface, but grade and environment matter.
- **Finishes:** MS may be supplied with mill-scale or pickled/oiled surfaces depending on type. SS finishes can include 2B, brushed No.4, mirror and hairline, depending on grade and supply.
- **Fabrication:** MS is suitable for many general fabrication operations; confirm grade and thickness. SS formability and fabrication suitability depend on grade, thickness, finish and process.
- **Common use cases:** MS is used for enclosures, tank fabrication, automotive parts, building cladding and general fabrication. SS may suit kitchen equipment, cladding, chemical equipment, elevator interiors and pharmaceutical tables, subject to suitability.
- **Lifecycle:** For MS, account for painting/coating, inspection and maintenance if corrosion protection is required. For SS, consider grade selection, cleaning, exposure and finish—not just the label “stainless”.

## When should you choose mild steel sheets?

MS sheets are often a practical choice when the component is designed for general fabrication and the project can accommodate a suitable corrosion-protection method. They are commonly considered for brackets, enclosures, tanks, automotive parts, cladding and fabricated structures.

Before ordering, confirm:

- Whether hot-rolled (HR) or cold-rolled (CR) sheet is required.
- The grade or specification required by the drawing or application.
- Thickness, sheet dimensions and allowable tolerances.
- Whether the surface will be painted, powder-coated, pickled or otherwise treated.
- Cutting, shearing, bending, welding and finishing requirements.
- Whether the material will be exposed to moisture, chemicals or outdoor conditions.

HR and CR sheets are not interchangeable for every job. Surface condition, dimensional requirements and fabrication needs should guide the choice. If the sheet will be coated after fabrication, include coating preparation and process requirements in the quotation request.

## When should you choose stainless steel sheets?

Stainless steel sheets are worth considering when corrosion resistance, cleanliness, surface appearance or maintenance requirements are important to the application. Stainless steel is not corrosion-proof in every environment: chloride exposure, chemicals, temperature, surface condition and design details can affect performance.

Stellar Global Supplies lists SS 202, 304 and 316 sheet options, with finishes including 2B, No.4, mirror and hairline. The correct grade and finish should be selected for the actual service environment and project specification.

Common application areas listed for stainless sheets include:

- Kitchen and food-related equipment, where the required grade and hygienic design are confirmed.
- Elevator interiors and architectural cladding where appearance and finish matter.
- Pharmaceutical tables and equipment, subject to project cleanliness and material requirements.
- Chemical equipment, after confirming compatibility with the process media.
- Fabricated panels and components requiring a specified stainless finish.

Grade 304 is commonly used for many general-purpose stainless applications, while 316 may be considered for more demanding chloride or chemical environments. Neither should be selected without checking the exposure conditions and relevant engineering requirements.

## How to compare cost correctly

The lowest material quote is not always the lowest overall project cost. Compare the total cost of the finished component, not only the sheet price.

Consider:

1. **Material price and quantity:** Calculate the required sheet area, thickness, cutting layout and expected scrap.
2. **Fabrication:** Include cutting, shearing, bending, welding, finishing and any process-specific handling.
3. **Surface protection:** MS may require coating or painting depending on use; include preparation, coating and maintenance.
4. **Service environment:** Evaluate moisture, chemicals, salt, cleaning agents, temperature and exposure.
5. **Maintenance and replacement:** Consider inspection, cleaning, repair, downtime and expected service life.
6. **Availability and delivery:** Confirm grade, thickness, sheet size, quantity and delivery timing with the supplier.

For a fair comparison, ask suppliers to quote the same material specification, thickness, dimensions, finish, quantity and delivery terms.

## A practical checklist before placing an MS or SS sheet order

Use this checklist when preparing your RFQ:

- Material: MS or SS, with grade/specification stated.
- Sheet type: HR, CR or required stainless grade.
- Thickness and tolerance.
- Sheet size or cut dimensions.
- Surface finish and coating requirements.
- Application and operating environment.
- Fabrication steps, including welding, bending or forming.
- Quantity and delivery location.
- Required inspection documents or material certificates.
- Target delivery date and packing requirements.

A clear RFQ helps suppliers respond accurately and reduces the risk of quoting a material that does not match the drawing or application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is MS sheet cheaper than stainless steel sheet?

Mild steel is often chosen when initial material cost is a major factor, but prices vary by grade, thickness, quantity and market conditions. Compare the full fabricated and maintained cost of the application rather than relying on a general price assumption.

### Does stainless steel sheet rust?

Stainless steel is corrosion-resistant, not universally rust-proof. Certain environments—including chloride exposure, contamination, crevices or unsuitable chemical conditions—can cause staining or corrosion. Grade, surface finish, design and maintenance all matter.

### What is the difference between HR and CR MS sheets?

Hot-rolled and cold-rolled sheets have different processing histories and surface characteristics. The choice should reflect dimensional, surface and fabrication requirements. Confirm the specification on your drawing or with your material engineer.

### Which stainless steel grade should I choose: 202, 304 or 316?

The answer depends on the environment, corrosion exposure, forming and welding requirements, finish, specification and budget. Do not choose a grade only by price or by a broad application label. Confirm suitability with the project specification and qualified technical advice.
